B2 · Sum-form relative Y

B2 · Sum-form relative Y
Stage2 — per sensor pair
InputCommon windows from B1; levelled forward velocity and yaw rate
OutputPairwise Δpy, its standard error, segment count
Core relationΔIk = −Δpy Wk
EstimatorWeighted robust linear regression over segments
Minimum dataTwo associated segments; design rank at least two

Two sensors on the same turning platform travel concentric arcs. Over one complete turn their path-length difference is proportional to the heading change and to their lateral separation — and the unknown vehicle speed cancels out entirely.

1 The same lever arm, now longitudinal

A4 used the lateral component of the rigid-body velocity relation. B2 uses the longitudinal one: a sensor mounted to the left or right of the rotation centre travels faster or slower along the vehicle's forward direction during a turn.

(B2.1)vj,x = vc,x − ω py,jThe platform speed vc,x is common to every sensor and unknown, which is exactly why a single sensor cannot solve for its own lateral offset.

Differencing two sensors at the same instant removes the common speed and leaves the lateral separation. That instantaneous form, however, requires the two sensors to be compared sample by sample — every sample then carries the full weight of any clock error.

(B2.2)Δvx = vi,x − vj,x = −ω ( py,i − py,j ) = −ω ΔpyCorrect, but sample-wise: this is the formulation NH-Calib deliberately does not use.

2 Integrating instead of differencing

Integrating both sides over the common window turns a per-sample constraint into one measurement per segment. Timing error then acts only at the two boundaries, which B1 has already interpolated.

(B2.3)Ii,k = ∫t0,kt1,k vi,x(t) dt,    Ij,k = ∫t0,kt1,k vj,x(t) dt,    Wk = ∫t0,kt1,k ω(t) dt = ΔθkForward distance travelled by each sensor over the identical window, and the platform's accumulated rotation in radians.
(B2.4)ΔIk = Ii,k − Ij,k = − Δpy,ij WkThe arc-length difference of two concentric arcs is the radius difference times the swept angle. Sign convention: ΔI = Ii − Ij paired with Δpy,ij = py,i − py,j.
Why this is the central trick
The vehicle's forward speed profile — unknown, time-varying, and shared — disappears completely from (B2.4). What remains is a straight line through the origin whose slope is the quantity being calibrated. No speed sensor, no map, and no overlapping field of view is involved.

3 Regression over segments

Each associated segment contributes one point (Wk, ΔIk). Collecting them across the drive turns calibration into a one-dimensional slope-fitting problem.

(B2.5)Zk = py Wk + β Tk + εk,     wk = |Wk| p−2A two-parameter weighted fit. The second term, proportional to segment duration Tk, absorbs a slowly varying scale discrepancy between the two channels so that it is not mistaken for lateral offset. With the default weight exponent p = 0.5 the weight is |Wk|−1.5.
  • At least two associated segments are required and the design matrix must have rank two; otherwise the pair is rejected rather than reported with a fabricated confidence.
  • Large |Wk| segments carry more slope information, which is why weighting is expressed in terms of accumulated heading rather than duration or sample count.
  • Both turn directions should be represented. A drive that only ever turns one way still identifies the slope, but a drift between channels aliases into it far more easily.
  • Because each segment gives one independent measurement, the standard error scales with the number of valid turns — which is why a drive with more turning windows reports a tighter result.
Representative value
A sensor pair produces one measurement per segment, and those measurements scatter. The pair's reported value is the robust aggregate, obtained with an M-estimator. Alternatives — plain least squares, RANSAC, median — were compared and the choice of aggregator was not the dominant error source.

4 What limits the accuracy

The estimator consumes odometry, so its accuracy is bounded by how differently the two channels' odometry drifts inside a window. Feeding ground-truth trajectories into the same estimator produces sub-millimetre agreement, which localises the residual error in the front end rather than in the formulation.

  • Per-window scatter is the dominant term, and it does not shrink by reweighting or by re-detecting windows — both were tested and neither helps.
  • It does shrink with more independent turns. Pooling several logs of the same platform reduced the reported lateral error substantially on the Ford configuration.
  • Common-window construction removes boundary mismatch but not differential drift inside the window; that part is a genuine lower bound set by the odometry.
  • The same lower bound applies to interval-based hand–eye methods, which consume the same integrated motion. Only methods that register raw point clouds against each other avoid it — at the cost of requiring overlapping fields of view.
