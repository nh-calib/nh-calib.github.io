A1 · Turn-segment extraction

A1 · Turn-segment extraction
Stage1 — per sensor, no association
InputRaw twist ξj(t) = [v⊤, ω⊤]⊤; optional platform speed vc
OutputOrdered segment list with start/end time, net heading Wk, duration Tk, sign, peak rate
Consumed byA2, A4, B1, B2
Runs beforeRoll/pitch levelling — detection is yaw-centric and pre-level
FailureZero accepted segments → channel dropped at stage turn

Every later estimator consumes the same list of turning segments. A1 smooths each sensor twist, then cuts it into direction-consistent turns that carry enough rotational information to be worth estimating from.

1 Why segments at all

NH-Calib never uses the whole drive. Straight driving carries almost no information about mounting angles or lever arms: the rotation axis is undefined when ω ≈ 0, and the lateral lever-arm term ωpx vanishes with it. Every NH-Calib observation equation is proportional to rotation, so the estimator is restricted to intervals that actually rotate.

Using a shared segment list also keeps the modules comparable. A2 fits a rotation axis, A4 fits a lateral residual and B2 integrates forward displacement — if each re-detected its own intervals, a disagreement between modules could not be traced to a single cause. Segments are detected once, in A1, and injected downstream.

Key point
Segments are detected before roll/pitch levelling. Detection therefore cannot depend on the attitude estimate that A2 has not produced yet. This is safe under the planar assumption: a significant rotation episode on a road vehicle is a yaw turn, so a yaw-centric scalar is a sufficient detector even in an unlevelled sensor frame.

2 Step 1 — centred twist smoothing

Scan-matching odometry produces trajectories that look clean at the second scale but whose frame-to-frame twist oscillates with period two. A4 and B2 read the twist directly, so that oscillation would be absorbed into the lever arms. A1 therefore smooths the twist before anything else touches it.

Nw = odd_round( τsmooth / median(Δt) ),   Nw ≥ 1,   half = (Nw − 1) / 2The smoothing window is specified as a time constant and converted to an odd sample count per dataset. At 30 Hz a 1.0 s constant gives 31 samples; at 10 Hz it gives 11. Nw = 1 is the identity.
Causal filters are prohibited
A trailing (causal) average delays yaw rate relative to lateral velocity. The relative-Y regression then reads that phase lag as a lever arm of the wrong sign. Only centred windows are allowed: the window [i−h, i+h] is symmetric, and shrinks symmetrically at the sequence ends.
  • Smoothing is applied to vx, vy, vz, wx, wy, wz only. Timestamps, dt and front-end quality fields are passed through unchanged.
  • Dropped-scan gaps split the stream into contiguous blocks; integration and smoothing never cross a gap.
  • Platform speed from chassis/CAN is not smoothed here — it is already a filtered vehicle signal.
  • No additional low-pass filtering is permitted elsewhere in the pipeline. Smoothing exists once, in A1.

3 Step 2 — turn detection

Detection runs on a single signed scalar per channel, the yaw-centric turn rate.

ωturn(t) = ‖ω(t)‖ · sign( ωz(t) )Default norm mode: magnitude from the full three-dimensional angular velocity, sign from its vertical component. A wz mode using the signed vertical component alone is available, and is used automatically for planar streams that carry no wx/wy.
1

Two-level hysteresis

A candidate opens when |ωturn| crosses the peak threshold and stays open while it remains above the lower hold threshold. One threshold alone either fragments a single turn into pieces or admits noise crossings.

2

Split on discontinuity

A candidate is cut where sample indices are non-contiguous, where sign(ω) changes, or where the sample spacing exceeds the maximum gap. The gap rule is mandatory: without it a whole drive can fuse into one mega-segment whose integral is meaningless.

3

Accept on accumulated rotation

A surviving candidate is kept only if its net heading change |Wk| reaches the minimum and the mean platform speed is above the speed floor. Low-speed manoeuvres give poor lever-arm leverage.

4

Inspect and exclude

Accepted segments get stable indices so that a human or an automatic quality rule can remove specific turns without changing the detector. Exclusion is a mask, not a re-detection.

4 Outputs

Implementation
Changing any A1 setting — including the smoothing window alone — invalidates everything downstream, because the detection signal itself changed. The pipeline recomputes from A1 rather than patching later modules. Odometry is not regenerated.
