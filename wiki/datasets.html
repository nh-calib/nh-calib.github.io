What was run on what. Sensor layout, ego-motion front end, time handling, reference channel and exclusions for each of the three public datasets used in the reported evaluation.

1 Why these three

The three datasets were not chosen to be easy. They were chosen because they place the method in three different observational regimes, and the method behaves differently in each — which is the part of the evaluation that carries information.

Note
A2D2 is real sensor data, not simulation. No in-house vehicle experiment is part of the reported results.

2 Ego-motion input per dataset

NH-Calib consumes a metric twist, not points. What produces that twist differs by dataset, and the difference matters when reading the results table.

1

A2D2 and Ford — LiDAR odometry only

Continuous poses are estimated with KISS-ICP and differentiated to give linear and angular velocity. No external signal enters the estimate.

2

RadarScenes — Doppler plus CAN

Geometric radar odometry was not reliable enough in the tested configuration, so Doppler ego-velocity is combined with a temporally aligned CAN yaw rate. This is an alternative input path, and the manuscript reports it as such rather than as a sensor-only five-degree-of-freedom result.

3

Sign gauge is never taken from CAN

In every dataset the rotation-axis sign is a gauge convention anchored to the chosen reference sensor. See A3 · Rotation-sign gauge.

Caution
Because the RadarScenes twist depends on an external CAN signal, alignment error against that signal propagates into X. The usability and the independence limits of that path are reported together; it is not interchangeable with the LiDAR-only results.

3 Front-end settings

Key point
The A2D2 fixed 30 Hz interval is not a convenience approximation. The stored intervals contain an alternating error, so both the time axis and the velocity reconstructed from pose increments are corrected consistently. Using the stored intervals directly corrupts every downstream velocity.

4 RadarScenes: alternating offset and calibration

For RadarScenes the time offset and the calibration are updated alternately for three iterations before the final two-dimensional evaluation. Roll and Pitch are not estimation targets for this dataset, because the Doppler-plus-CAN input is two-dimensional by construction. The final run estimated a slip coefficient of c = 0.0049.

5 What was excluded, and why it is reported

  • Ford Log2 and Log3 did not contain enough valid turn segments and were excluded from calibration. They are reported as applicability failures rather than silently dropped — a drive with too little turning is outside the method's operating regime, and hiding that would misstate the regime.
  • Ford segment acceptance requires the input angular-rate cosine gate and the normalised error gate to pass; a drive is rejected when too few valid associated turns survive. See Gates and failure modes.
  • Ford Log4, Log5 and Log6 use identical timestamp handling and KISS-ICP settings but are scored as three independent evaluation sets. Neither the minimum across Logs nor a pooled value is presented as representing Ford as a whole.
Caution
Ford INS and official ground-truth poses may share a positioning source. They are therefore not interpreted as an independent motion reference.

6 Where ground truth is and is not used

All main-result cells are ground-truth-free during estimation. Official extrinsics enter only at scoring time, through the reference-sensor anchor described on the results page. Segment acceptance and graph weights are determined without ground truth. The one experiment that does inject ground truth into the pipeline — the attitude oracle — is labelled separately and is a diagnostic, not a result.
