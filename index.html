NH-Calib: Target-Free Multi-Sensor Extrinsic Calibration from Nonholonomic Vehicle Motion

NH-Calib estimates relative 5-DoF extrinsics from independently measured sensor ego-motion, using the vehicle's nonholonomic motion instead of targets, scene overlap, or ground-truth calibration.

Paper · under review Code · after review Explore the method

Authors and affiliations are intentionally withheld for double-anonymous review.

Abstract

Extrinsic calibration without targets, overlap, or ground-truth calibration.

Accurate extrinsic parameters are essential for fusing vehicle-mounted sensors in a common frame, yet distributed sensors may lack the shared scene structure required for direct registration. NH-Calib uses locally planar driving and the rear-axle nonholonomic constraint to separate sensor-independent estimation from cross-sensor association. Each sensor's own metric twist determines X, roll, pitch, and yaw; Z remains unobservable under planar motion, while only relative Y is estimated from associated turning segments. For relative Y, a sum-form relation differences longitudinal integrated motion over a common window, cancelling the vehicle-common speed component and reducing sensitivity to frame-level time offsets. We evaluate absolute 4-DoF and relative 5-DoF calibration errors, baselines, and ablations using LiDAR and radar configurations from A2D2, RadarScenes, and Ford Multi-AV. Ground-truth extrinsics are used only for evaluation and never by the estimator.

Overview

A common vehicle motion is the calibration signal.

Multi-sensor systems normally require calibration targets, overlapping fields of view, or direct cross-sensor registration. Those assumptions become fragile when sensors use different modalities, observe different parts of the scene, or are mounted far apart.

NH-Calib instead exploits the shared rigid-body motion of sensors mounted on a road vehicle. The nonholonomic constraint separates what can be estimated per sensor from the one translation component that requires pairwise turn association.

Method

Two stages, one reference sensor.

Stage 1 estimates per-sensor quantities and removes their common component by reference-relative differencing. Stage 2 estimates only relative Y from associated turns and consolidates pairwise measurements in a sensor graph.

01

Per-sensor estimation

Extract turns, align roll and pitch, then estimate X and yaw from each sensor's metric twist.

02

Reference-relative output

Subtract the chosen reference sensor so vehicle-common components cancel without requiring vehicle-frame ground truth.

03

Pairwise Y graph

Associate turns, use a common time window, estimate pairwise Y with sum-form integration, and solve a Y-only graph.

Experiments

Cross-modal validation without GT in estimation.

Ground-truth extrinsics are used only for final evaluation. Reported errors are mean absolute errors over valid directed sensor pairs; lower is better.
